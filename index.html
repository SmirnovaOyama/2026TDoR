Transgender Day of Remembrance

On 20 November, vigils around the world read the names of trans people killed in the previous twelve months. TGEU's Trans Murder Monitoring recorded 273 between 1 October 2025 and 30 September 2026.1

Who was killed

Most of those killed were trans women, and most were Black or Brown. Seven in ten killings were in Latin America and the Caribbean.1

People reported killed this year

      Undercounting

      A death is counted only if it is reported and the person is known to have been trans.

      In the United States, coroners' reports and death certificates do not record whether a person was trans.2 Some families do not disclose it after a death, and some people are recorded under a name they no longer used.

      This year's total is under 300 for only the second time in a decade. TGEU attributes this partly to harder conditions for reporting: less civic space, hostility from media and governments, and funding cuts.1

      Suicide among trans women in China

      TGEU counts murders only, so deaths by suicide appear in none of the figures above. Many vigils remember both. In mainland China, trans communities keep their own records of the people they have lost, many of them young trans women who died by suicide.

      56%

      Of 1,309 trans adults in a national survey in China had thought about suicide at some point, and 16% had attempted it. Intense conflict with parents was linked to both.9

      2022

      From December, China stopped online sales of cyproterone, a hormone blocker many trans women relied on because prescriptions are hard to get.10 Activists told RFA they saw more suicides afterwards, and some people were cheated by black-market sellers.11

      One Among Us keeps a memorial site, 那些秋叶, with pages for Chinese-speaking trans people who have died and messages left by visitors: one-among.us.12

      If you are in mainland China and need someone to talk to, call 12356, the national psychological assistance hotline.13

      Chosen names

      For trans young people, each more place where they could use their chosen name went with 29% fewer suicidal thoughts and 56% less suicidal behaviour.3

      A study of 129 trans and gender-nonconforming young people in three US cities, asking about home, school, work and friends

      Other studies of trans young people find similar associations with support and care.

      73%

      Lower odds of suicidal thoughts or self-harm among young people who started puberty blockers or hormones, over about a year of follow-up at a Seattle clinic4

      34%

      Lower odds of a suicide attempt in the past year for trans and nonbinary young people of colour with high support from family5

      These studies show associations and cannot prove cause. Their samples are small or self-selected. They still point the same way.

      History

      1. 28 November 1998

        Rita Hester, a Black trans woman, is killed in her apartment in Allston, Massachusetts. Newspapers report her death under a name she no longer used and call her a man. Her murder has never been solved.6 8

      2. February 1999

        Gwendolyn Ann Smith starts Remembering Our Dead, a website listing trans people lost to violence. She bases its design on the Vietnam Veterans Memorial, a wall of names.6

      3. November 1999

        About a hundred people hold a candlelight vigil in San Francisco and read the names aloud. It becomes the first Transgender Day of Remembrance.6

      4. 2009

        TGEU starts the Trans Murder Monitoring project, with records reaching back to 2008.7

      5. 2026

        Vigils are held in many countries. The count reaches 5,592.7

      Find a vigil

      Vigils are held on and around 20 November. Local trans groups, LGBTQ+ centres and Pride organisations usually list them. TGEU gathers the year's materials and the full monitoring data.
